## Clinical Context and Motivation

Clinical genomics laboratories operate under increasing diagnostic pressure, requiring fast, accurate, and  clinically interpretable results while managing increasingly large and complex genomic datasets.
Rare disease diagnostics remains one of the major challenges in clinical genomics due to the heterogeneity of genomic alterations, variable phenotypic presentations, and the limitations of standard analytical pipelines.

Critical analytical steps such as structural variant analysis, mitochondrial DNA characterization, and phenotype-driven variant prioritization have a direct impact on diagnostic yield. However, each of these components presents methodological limitations that can hinder consistent and reproducible interpretation in routine clinical diagnostics.
Improvements in these areas can increase diagnostic yield and reduce the manual burden associated with genomic analysis by streamlining variant interpretation.

During my work in a hospital genomics environment, I identified several opportunities to strengthen analytical performance and workflow robustness, including:
- Integration of complementary structural variant callers to improve sensitivity and breakpoint resolution  
- Refined detection and interpretation of mitochondrial DNA alterations
- Optimization of phenotype‑driven variant prioritization strategies  
- Improved reproducibility, modularity, and scalability of diagnostic workflows

One of the main challenges identified during routine clinical analysis was the large number of candidate variants requiring manual review after exome sequencing.

Reducing the interpretation burden while maintaining recovery of clinically relevant variants became a major motivation for evaluating alternative prioritization and filtering strategies.

To address these challenges, this repository documents workflow optimization proposals for structural variant and mitochondrial DNA analysis, together with a real-world benchmarking study of phenotype-driven variant prioritization strategies performed using previously diagnosed clinical cases.
